The Nebius day

One of Noetic's one-day builds, and what a day has to be designed for.

Role
Co-founder, Noetic
When
2026

Visit Noetic

The question

A hackathon is a learning environment that mostly refuses to admit it is one. People arrive with very different amounts of practice, get scored against each other on the polish of a build, and the ones who learned the most often leave with the least to show.

Noetic runs one-day builds where people work with a frontier company's newest tools and that company's engineers are in the room. The Nebius day was one of them. The design question was how to run a competitive day that does not quietly sort people by how much they already knew when they walked in.

My approach

Two decisions carried most of the weight.

Tracks are split by experience, so a first-timer is never scored against someone who has already shipped. The split is about what a score means: a beginner losing to an expert learns nothing from the result.

Judging rewards whether a team can explain why they chose a model, ahead of how finished the build looks. Polish on a one-day build mostly measures prior practice. The explanation is the part that is actually about the day.

Evidence, and what it does not show

103 people applied, 63 were selected, and all 63 came.

Those are participation figures. A full room says the day was worth travelling to and that selection and reminders worked. It says nothing about whether anyone understood a model choice better at six o'clock than at nine, and nothing about what any of them did the following week. I did not collect that, so I cannot claim it.

This case study is still being written. What is above is the whole of what is claimed so far; the sections still to come are the detail, not a different story.
