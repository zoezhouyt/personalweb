Where it starts

Three accents

My English changed shape three times. In Beijing I spoke American, copied from a teacher. In Abu Dhabi, with stretches in Riyadh, I could not copy the accent at all, so I copied something else: the manner of the Emirati women who were friends of my parents, who were courteous to everyone and certain when they spoke.

Those women mentored me. They asked what I wanted to study, what I should go and see, whether I should start something at university, and what I would need to show before anyone took an idea of mine seriously.

Moving that often teaches you that the same explanation does not land the same way twice. It is why I am wary of a learning design that works in one classroom and is assumed to travel.

2026

LSE, three labs

The question I keep returning to

Research

Calibration is the fit between how confident a learner is and how well they actually perform. Generative AI has made it urgent: a fluent answer feels like understanding, and that feeling arrives whether or not the understanding does.

  • DissertationDr Luc Schneider

    When an AI assistant supports a learner's own judgement, and when it quietly replaces the work that would have built it.

  • Curiosity and misinformationDr Yana Litovsky

    Guess generation and susceptibility to misleading claims. It made me question one of my own design decisions, which is in the next leg.

  • Digital safety for at-risk youthDr Sakshi Ghai

    Mapped which interventions might suit young people in Mukuru, Kenya. An intervention can be coherent inside a framework and still miss how a community meets risk.

Three prototypes

Building for learners

Two of them stopped on purpose

The first ran into a problem I had not considered. The second into one I had, and had got backwards.

  • A reflective chatbotStopped

    Started at Stanford GSE's AI Tinkery. A father told me he was relieved: he had been out of school for years and could not answer his child's questions, and now something could. His relief unsettled me. I had built a tool a parent could use to step back.

  • A story-relay gameStopped

    Adapted from a Berkeley project. I set it aside before any child used it. Extending a story rewards building on an idea, and the habit I wanted to train was questioning one.

  • Miku, a teachable agentCurrent

    The learner teaches Miku, who is deliberately blank and answers only from what it has been taught, then predicts question by question what Miku will get right. The distance between prediction and result is the calibration measure.

  • Where Miku standsHonest status

    Adult friends have used it. I am interviewing parents before any child does. No child data yet, so no claim that it works.

  • LinkSeaHong Kong

    Built the student-facing interface of a school workflow platform in JavaScript, field-tested it with the students, and rebuilt the parts they could not complete.
