Miku

Founder A teachable AI that helps learners practise metacognitive calibration.

How it works. The learner teaches Miku, an AI pupil that starts blank and answers only from what it has been taught. Before each question, the learner predicts whether Miku will get it right. The gap between prediction and result is the calibration measure.

Where it came from. Two earlier versions, a reflection chatbot and a story-relay game, both aimed at helping children reflect on their own thinking.

Where it stands. Adult friends have used it. I’m interviewing parents before any child does.

LSE, Department of Psychological and Behavioural Science

Research assistant Two research projects on how people judge what they know.

Dr Yana Litovsky: curiosity, guessing and misinformation. How guessing and processing fluency affect susceptibility to misleading claims. It made me ask whether Miku’s prediction step anchors learners to their first judgement.

Dr Sakshi Ghai: digital safety for young people in Kenya. I reviewed digital-safety interventions and mapped which might suit young people in Mukuru. It made me rethink my first chatbot, which invited children to confide in an AI.
